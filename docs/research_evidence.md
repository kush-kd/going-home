# Verified evidence behind the Going Home impact thesis

Four research passes (2026-10-24). Each figure was read from the source: PubMed records via NCBI E-utilities, full text on PMC or JAMA, or the official report or page. Confidence is rated high, medium or low. Anything flagged as unverified is **not used** in the report.

## A. Harm from medicines after discharge
| # | Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 19% of patients (76/400) had an adverse event after discharge; 66% of these were adverse drug events. 6% of patients had a preventable event and 6% an ameliorable one. Of the 25 events that caused at least temporary disability, 48% were preventable. | Forster AJ et al. Ann Intern Med 2003;138:161-7. PMID 12558354 | high |
| A2 | About 99,628 emergency hospitalizations a year for adverse drug events in adults aged 65+ (2007–09). Warfarin, insulins, oral antiplatelets and oral hypoglycemics together caused 67.0% of them; 65.7% were unintentional overdoses. | Budnitz DS et al. NEJM 2011;365:2002-12. PMID 22111719 | high |
| A3 | 6.1 ED visits per 1,000 people a year for medication harms (2017–19), 12.1 per 1,000 for ages 65+; 38.6% led to hospitalization. Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) caused 5.9% of visits and warfarin 8.4%. | Budnitz DS et al. JAMA 2021;326:1299-1309. PMC8493432 | high |
| A4 | About 1.27 million ED visits for oral-anticoagulant bleeding over 2016–20; 47.8% hospitalized. Apixaban was involved in 52.4% of DOAC bleeding visits. | Geller AI et al. Thromb Res 2023;225:110-5. PMC10870325 | high |
| A5 | More than 1.5 million ED visits a year for adverse drug events; almost 500,000 hospitalizations. | CDC Medication Safety pages (2024) | high |
| A6 | 14.1% of older patients had a medication discrepancy at home; 50.8% of contributing factors were patient-related. 30-day readmission was 14.3% with a discrepancy vs 6.1% without. | Coleman EA et al. Arch Intern Med 2005;165:1842-7. PMID 16157827 | high |
| A7 | Patients had no understanding of 81.6% of their stopped medicines and 69.3% of their dose changes. | Ziaeian B et al. J Gen Intern Med 2012;27:1513-20. PMID 22798200 | high |
| A8 | Medication errors in about half of heart patients after discharge; 59.2% misunderstood the purpose, dose or frequency of a cardiac medicine. | Mixon AS et al. Mayo Clin Proc 2014;89:1042-51. PMID 24998906 | high |
| A9 | Pharmacist counseling at discharge: preventable ADEs at 30 days fell from 11% to 1%. | Schnipper JL et al. Arch Intern Med 2006;166:565-71. PMID 16534045 | high |
| A10 | Deaths: 106,000 fatal adverse drug reactions in hospitalized patients (1994, extrapolated and contested); 9,856 deaths from acknowledged prescription errors (1998 death certificates, likely undercounted). | Lazarou 1998 JAMA PMID 9555760; Phillips 2002 PMID 11910058 | medium |

## B. Language
| # | Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 49.1% of adverse events in limited-English (LEP) patients caused physical harm, vs 29.5% for English speakers. Communication errors caused 52.4% vs 35.9%. | Divi C et al. Int J Qual Health Care 2007;19:60-7. PMID 17277013 | high |
| B2 | Non-English speakers had higher odds of 30-day readmission (OR 1.3); OR 1.7 for Chinese speakers and 1.5 for Spanish speakers. | Karliner LS et al. J Hosp Med 2010;5:276-82. PMID 20533573 | high |
| B3 | Heart failure: 30-day ED visit 21.6% LEP vs 14.7% (RR 1.32). COPD: 30-day readmission 15.6% vs 11.8% (RR 1.51). | Rawal S et al. JAMA 2019;322:1605-7 | high |
| B4 | LEP patients knew their medication category less often (45% vs 54%; adjusted OR 0.63) and their appointment type less often (50% vs 66%). | Karliner LS et al. Med Care 2012;50:283-9. PMID 22411441 | high |
| B5 | An interpreter phone at every bedside cut 30-day readmission for LEP patients from 17.8% to 13.4% (about 119 readmissions avoided). | Karliner LS et al. Med Care 2017;55:199-206. PMID 27579909 | high |
| B6 | Ad hoc interpreters (family, including an 11-year-old): 63% of errors could have clinical consequences, including omitted dose, frequency and duration. 43% of LEP patients still had family interpret at discharge even after interpreter phones were installed. | Flores G et al. Pediatrics 2003. PMID 12509547; Lee JS et al. Patient Educ Couns 2018. PMID 28774652 | high |
| B7 | Machine translation of discharge instructions: Google Translate was 92% accurate in Spanish and 81% in Chinese, with 2% and 8% of sentences potentially harmful. GPT-4 was 97% and 95% accurate, with ≤1% of sentences potentially harmful. | Khoong EC et al. JAMA Intern Med 2019; Kong 2026 BMJ Qual Saf. PMID 40633961 | high |
| B8 | Counter-evidence: written Spanish instructions alone did not raise comprehension scores (n=20), though 91% preferred their own language. | Jang M et al. 2018. PMID 29958619 | high |
| B9 | 26.5 million LEP people in the US (2022, 8.4%). Top languages: Spanish 17.6M, Chinese 1.82M, Vietnamese 0.90M, Korean 0.56M, Tagalog 0.54M (over 75% together). | Migration Policy Institute, Hofstetter testimony, March 2025 | high |

## C. Comprehension, schedules, reminders, warning signs
| # | Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | 36% of US adults have basic or below-basic health literacy. | Kutner M et al. NCES 2006-483 (NAAL 2003) | high |
| C2 | 78% of ED patients had deficient comprehension of discharge instructions, and they recognized it only 20% of the time. | Engel KG et al. Ann Emerg Med 2009. PMID 18619710 | high |
| C3 | Only 43.9% of older patients recalled the details of their scheduled appointment; 59.6% could describe their diagnosis. | Horwitz LI et al. JAMA Intern Med 2013. PMID 23958851 | high |
| C4 | Low health literacy: adjusted IRR 1.46 for 30-day readmission or ED return. | Mitchell SE et al. J Health Commun 2012. PMID 23030580 | high |
| C5 | Project RED (randomized): 30-day hospital use 0.314 vs 0.451 visits per person per month (IRR 0.695). Its After Hospital Care Plan had "an appointment calendar, a color-coded medication schedule... and information about what to do if a problem arises." | Jack BW et al. Ann Intern Med 2009. PMC2738592 | high |
| C6 | Care Transitions Intervention: 30-day readmission 8.3% vs 11.9%. | Coleman EA et al. Arch Intern Med 2006. PMID 17000937 | high |
| C7 | Without a standard schedule, only 14.9% of adults organized a 7-drug regimen into 4 or fewer daily times. | Wolf MS et al. Arch Intern Med 2011. PMID 21357804 | high |
| C8 | A label with standard times of day improved correct use (85.9% vs 77.4%), with the largest effect in limited literacy (OR 5.08). | Wolf MS et al. J Gen Intern Med 2016. PMID 27542666 | high |
| C9 | Text-message reminders: OR 2.11 for adherence (about 50% to 68%). | Thakkar J et al. JAMA Intern Med 2016. PMID 26831740 | high |
| C10 | Plain-language pictogram dosing sheets: dosing errors 5.4% vs 47.8%. | Yin HS et al. Arch Pediatr Adolesc Med 2008. PMID 18762597 | high |
| C11 | Heart failure: a weight gain of more than 2–5 lb in the week before admission carries adjusted OR 2.77, and more than 10 lb OR 7.65; the rise begins at least a week before admission. | Chaudhry SI et al. Circulation 2007. PMID 17846286 | high |
| C12 | An untreated stroke destroys 1.9 million neurons per minute. | Saver JL. Stroke 2006. PMID 16339467 | high |
| C13 | Early follow-up after heart failure discharge: lower 30-day readmission (HR 0.85; observational). | Hernandez AF et al. JAMA 2010. PMID 20442387 | high |
| C14 | Stroke-symptom education campaigns: 10 of 13 studies reported significantly shorter delays before reaching hospital (quality varied; no meta-analysis). | Mellon L et al. Patient Relat Outcome Meas 2015;6:61-73. PMID 25750550 | medium |

## D. Scale and cost
| # | Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | About 35.5 million US hospital stays a year (2016–19). | HCUP Statistical Brief #306 | med-high |
| D2 | 30-day readmission rate 13.9 per 100; 3,850,413 readmissions in 2020; average cost $16,300. | HCUP Statistical Brief #304 | high |
| D3 | Median 27.1% of readmissions avoidable; 26.9% potentially preventable in a US national cohort. | van Walraven 2011 CMAJ; Auerbach 2016 JAMA Intern Med | high |
| D4 | Median 21% of readmissions drug-related, and a median 69% of those preventable (mostly non-US studies). | El Morabet N et al. J Am Geriatr Soc 2018. PMID 29468640 | high/med |
| D5 | Medicare's Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program penalties: about $521M in FY2022, with 2,499 hospitals penalized. | KFF Health News 2021; CMS HRRP page | high |
| D6 | 63 million US family caregivers. Of those doing medical tasks, 78% manage medications. Only 20% got at least 24 hours' notice of a discharge. | AARP/NAC 2025; Reinhard 2012 "Home Alone"; AARP 2019 | high |

**Not used (unverified):** the NAAL head count; the "FDA 1.3 million injured / one death a day" figure; the IOM "7,000 deaths from medication errors" figure; and any total for the share of harms caused by caregiver misunderstanding.
