Going Home

Hospital discharge papers, turned into a plan a family can actually follow.

Kush Dhall · Final project · https://github.com/kush-kd/going-home

Why I built this

Before Yale I worked on health programs in Cambodia, Indonesia and Laos. A lot of that work was translation: making a plan usable for the people who have to act on it.

Discharge is the most personal version of that problem. Mr. Chen, 79, goes home after a mini-stroke with 2 new medicines, 2 changed doses, 2 to stop, and 5 follow-up visits and tests, in shorthand like "1 tab PO BID". His daughter reads Chinese.

Going Home: built for the family, turns the plan into actions, and checks every dose against the source.

What the family gets

A day-by-day timetable in Chinese with a red STOP box, reminders on the phone, and a warning card that says when to call 911 and when to call the clinic.

How I built it

AI reads and explains. Code checks. The Reader (Opus) extracts every item with its source sentence. Code matches every dose, date and phone number. The Checker (Sonnet) uses its own tools to look for misses. The Explainer writes it in plain words in 11 languages.

It restates. It never recommends.

What it does

  • Restates the hospital's plan: what, how much, when, how long
  • Turns it into a timetable, reminders and a warning card
  • Asks the doctor when something is unclear

What it never does

  • Diagnose or give medical advice
  • Change a dose or add a medicine
  • Show a dose it couldn't match to the papers

Enforced by code: unmatched items stay off the timetable; any explanation with a new number, purpose or tip is dropped; the hospital's sentence is on every card.

Could it save lives?

Harm after discharge comes from a plan that doesn't survive the trip home. A randomized trial that gave patients a medication schedule, an appointment calendar and warning signs cut hospital returns by about 30% (Project RED). Going Home builds that plan automatically, in the family's language.

What I learned, and what's next

  • The worst bug was a safety filter that deleted "STOP aspirin". Checks need tests too.
  • Giving the Checker real tools made it cheaper and more careful.
  • Next: a pilot with discharge nurses, read-back questions, a back-translation check, and SMS reminders.
