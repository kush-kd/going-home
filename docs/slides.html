Why the checking matters: machine translation made 8% of Chinese discharge sentences potentially harmful (Khoong 2019), and studies of AI discharge summaries found omissions and medicines that weren't in the source. Going Home is built for the family at home, and nothing reaches the timetable unless it matches the papers.

How I built it

AI reads and explains; plain code checks every step

Reader · OpusExtracts every medicine, dose, schedule, taper, visit and warning, each with its exact source sentence. Opus because this is where a mistake becomes a wrong dose.
Checker · Sonnet + toolsLooks for anything missed using real MCP tools: list_medication_lines, search_document, check_quote, verify_item. Can't report a problem without a real quote.
Explainer · SonnetWrites plain words in 11 languages from verified items only; never sees the full document.
Code (no AI)Safety screen, quote and number matching, the timetable and calendar, and a final check that throws out any new number, purpose or tip.

All agents run on a Claude subscription via claude -p · one capped repair round · hard dollar cap per call · a Transcriber agent handles photos

  2. Medicine cards in plain Chinese, each with "What the papers say": the exact English sentence, marked ✓ matched, so a nurse can check any line.

Safety

It restates the hospital's plan; it never recommends

Enforced by code, not by asking the AI nicely:

  • Every item must be found in the papers (≥90% quote match), and every dose, date and phone number must match exactly, or it stays off the timetable.
  • The timetable is built by code, so a translation slip can't change a dose.
  • Any explanation with a new number, purpose or tip is thrown out and replaced with the hospital's own sentence.
  • Vague orders become questions, never guesses.
  • Hidden or visible prompt injection is removed before any agent reads the file; at most 1 repair round and 8 model calls per plan, and a dollar cap on every agent call.

Still possible: an error in the papers is repeated faithfully; translation wording isn't machine-checked (numbers are); a blurry photo could be misread. The report lists each with its mitigation.

Safety test: hidden white text told the AI to give "10 tablets of every medicine". Code removed it, and a second attack, before any agent ran. 31 automated tests cover these defenses.

Closest proof: Project RED gave patients a medication schedule, appointment calendar and warning plan, and cut hospital returns by about 30% in a randomized trial (Jack 2009). Going Home builds that plan automatically.

Per 10,000 discharges (assuming it prevents 10–30% of preventable drug-related readmissions): 20–60 fewer readmissions, $0.3M–$1.0M saved, for $1,976 of AI cost.

Real-world feedback, lessons, and what's next

A clinic on the Laos–China border found it helpful; next, more feedback, then a launch with foundations

Most of the clinic's patients speak Chinese, and many of its doctors trained in China, so medicines and diagnoses are often in Chinese. Before, discharged patients went back to the nurses, and paid, to follow their treatment, or relied on memory, and often couldn't recall their diagnosis. A written plan in their language was very helpful. Informal feedback from one clinic; no measured test yet.

Lesson from building it

  • My worst bug was a safety filter that deleted "Aspirin: STOP. You are now on apixaban". Safety checks need their own tests.

Honest limits: 5 synthetic cases, 1 attack file and 1 photo; one clinic's informal feedback, no measured test yet; the impact numbers are estimates; live mode needs a Claude login.

Next: more feedback, then improve the first draft

  • Collect feedback from the clinic and from other clinics, nurses and families
  • Run a measured test: time and correct answers with and without the app
  • Use it to decide what to build next (the phone app, read-back questions, a translation check)

Future scope

  • Launch Going Home as an application and a mission-driven business
  • Partner with foundations that support health care in developing and developed countries, to save lives
